Choosing a leather jacket manufacturer is not only about finding a price. A useful supplier conversation should establish what is being made, how the product will be checked, what the order includes and what each side needs to confirm before production.

This checklist is intended for retailers, clothing brands and buyers preparing to discuss a leather jacket project.

## 1. Prepare a clear product brief

Before asking for a quote, collect the details that define the product:

- Reference photos, sketches or an existing sample
- Intended fit and size range
- Colour and finish expectations
- Lining, hardware, pocket and closure details
- Branding, labels and packaging requirements
- Estimated quantity and size breakdown
- Destination country and target delivery window

If some specifications are not decided, mark them as open questions rather than assuming they are included.

## 2. Clarify the material specification

“Leather jacket” is not a complete material specification. Buyers should ask what leather type, thickness, finish, colour treatment and lining are intended. If the project requires a particular material, ask how it will be documented and approved.

Request a material sample or swatch where appropriate and confirm the approved reference before production.

## 3. Discuss sampling and approvals

Ask whether a sample can be produced, what it will cost, how revisions are handled and which details need written approval. A sample review should cover fit, measurements, stitching, hardware, colour, lining and finishing.

Record approved changes in one specification document so the production brief and approved sample remain aligned.

## 4. Agree on quality checks

Discuss checks that matter for your product, such as measurement tolerances, stitching consistency, hardware operation, colour consistency, lining attachment, labelling and final inspection.

Ask the manufacturer to explain which checks are available and how defects will be handled. Avoid assuming a certification or testing process exists unless confirmed.

## 5. Compare quotes on the same basis

A lower headline price may not include the same materials, accessories, packaging, sampling or inspection requirements. Ask each supplier to list what is included, what is excluded, payment terms, order quantity assumptions and proposed delivery terms.

For international orders, confirm shipping responsibilities, export documentation, duties and Incoterms with the relevant parties before committing.

## 6. Confirm production and communication expectations

Ask who will be the contact person, how changes are approved, when updates are shared and what happens if a material or component becomes unavailable. Any promised timeline should account for sample approval, material procurement, production and shipping.

## 7. Use a written checklist before placing an order

Before confirming an order, document the specification, sample approval, quantity, price, payment terms, timeline, inspection requirements, packaging and shipping responsibilities.

A good supplier relationship starts with clear expectations on both sides.
